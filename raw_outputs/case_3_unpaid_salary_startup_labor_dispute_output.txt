STANDING: MODERATE
SUMMARY: Withholding earned salary, full & final (F&F) settlement, or withholding relieving letters is prohibited under the Payment of Wages Act and Indian Contract Act. Non-compete clauses post-termination are void under Section 27.

=== STATUTORY ACTION ROADMAP ===

Step 2: Lodge a formal complaint before the Office of the Labour Commissioner / Labour Conciliation Officer in your jurisdiction.
Step 3: For managerial roles not covered under Labour Act, file a summary suit for debt recovery under Order 37 CPC.

2. STATUTORY VIOLATIONS & LEGAL GROUNDS:
Your aforesaid act of arbitrarily and unilaterally withholding the legitimate dues of the Undersigned is illegal, fraudulent, and amounts to actionable default under the laws of India, including:
• Violation of Payment of Wages Act, 1936 [Section 15 - Claims Arising out of Deductions from Wages or Delay in Payment]: Employers cannot withhold earned wages without explicit statutory authorization. Applies to employees earning up to statutory wage limits.
• Violation of Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 [Section 25F - Conditions Precedent to Retrenchment]: Requires 1 month notice or wages in lieu thereof, plus retrenchment compensation of 15 days average pay for each completed year of service.
• Violation of Indian Contract Act, 1872 [Section 27 - Agreement in Restraint of Trade Void]: Any clause restraining an employee from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business is legally void. Employers cannot withhold relieving letters or salaries based on illegal non-competes.
• Violation of Indian Contract Act, 1872 [Section 73 - Compensation for Loss or Damage Caused by Breach of Contract]: When a contract is broken, the party who suffers is entitled to receive compensation for any loss or damage naturally arising from the breach.
• Violation of Limitation Act, 1963 [Article 113 / General Schedule - Statutory Period of Limitation for Recovery]: General civil recovery suits must be filed within 3 years from the date payment became due. Consumer complaints have a 2-year limitation (Section 69 CPA).
